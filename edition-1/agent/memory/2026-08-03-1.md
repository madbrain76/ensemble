### The substantive insight (Bill's, sharpened)
Google's Confidential Space attests through closed-source Google-modified EDK2 firmware.
Therefore a tenant on Confidential Space cannot defend against Google. Publicly known,
almost nobody cares.

Two gaps in the industry-standard Confidential Inference story:
1. Threshold key release protects the key at rest and at release; once reconstructed
   inside the Trusted Loader, weights are plaintext in accelerator memory. The single
   point of failure moved, it didn't disappear.
2. THE LOAD-BEARING ONE: the attestation root is a monoculture. Jurisdictionally diverse
   key servers all verify signature chains terminating at one silicon vendor, measuring
   provider-supplied closed firmware. m-of-n collapses to 1-of-1.

The lever that makes anyone care is COMPLIANCE, not security. Under the AI Kill Switch Act
($20M/day for failing an emergency shutdown order, plus mandatory forensic preservation of
weights/telemetry), a kill switch enforced by unauditable third-party firmware is not a
kill switch, it's an assertion. And forensics produced by infrastructure you can't audit,
operated by an entity that may be in scope, aren't evidence. Security concerns get filed;
compliance defects get funded.
